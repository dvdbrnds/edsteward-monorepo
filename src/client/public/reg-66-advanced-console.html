17 U.S.C. § 110(2) - TEACH Act: Limitations on exclusive rights

§ 110(2) Distance Education Exception (TEACH Act)

The performance of a nondramatic literary or musical work or reasonable and limited portions of any other work, or display of a work in an amount comparable to that which is typically displayed in the course of a live classroom session, by or in the course of a transmission, if—

(A) the performance or display is made by, at the direction of, or under the actual supervision of an instructor as an integral part of a class session offered as a regular part of the systematic mediated instructional activities of a governmental body or an accredited nonprofit educational institution;
(B) the performance or display is directly related and of material assistance to the teaching content of the transmission;
(C) the transmission is made solely for, and, to the extent technologically feasible, the reception of such transmission is limited to—
(i) students officially enrolled in the course for which the transmission is made; or
(ii) officers or employees of governmental bodies as a part of their official duties or employment;
(D) the transmitting body or institution—
(i) institutes policies regarding copyright,
(ii) provides informational materials to faculty, students, and relevant staff members that accurately describe, and promote compliance with, the laws of the United States relating to copyright, and
(iii) provides notice to students that materials used in connection with the course may be subject to copyright protection;
(E) if the transmission is digital, the transmitting body or institution applies technological measures that reasonably prevent—
(i) retention of the work in accessible form by recipients of the transmission from the transmitting body or institution for longer than the class session; and
(ii) unauthorized further dissemination of the work in accessible form by such recipients to others; and
(F) the transmitting body or institution does not engage in conduct that could reasonably be expected to interfere with technological measures used by copyright owners to prevent such retention or unauthorized further dissemination.
§ 112(f) Ephemeral Recordings for Educational Transmissions

Notwithstanding the provisions of section 106, and without limiting the application of subsection (b), it is not an infringement of copyright for a governmental body or other nonprofit educational institution entitled under section 110(2) to transmit a performance or display to make copies or phonorecords of a work that is in digital form and, solely to the extent permitted in paragraph (2), of a work that is in analog form, embodying the performance or display to be used for making transmissions authorized under section 110(2), if—

(1) such copies or phonorecords are retained and used solely by the body or institution that made them, and no further copies or phonorecords are reproduced from them, except as authorized under section 110(2) and this subsection;
(2) such copies or phonorecords are used solely for transmissions authorized under section 110(2); and
(3) unless preserved exclusively for archival purposes, such copies or phonorecords are destroyed or deleted at the end of the class session for which the performance or display was transmitted.
Key Definitions
Mediated instructional activities: Activities that use such work as an integral part of the class experience, controlled by or under the actual supervision of the instructor and analogous to the type of performance or display that would take place in a live classroom setting.
Reasonable and limited portions: A quantitative limitation on the portion of any work that may be performed or displayed in a single course. For audiovisual works, this typically means a "reasonable and limited" portion rather than the entire work.
Technological measures: Technologies, processes, or procedures reasonably designed to prevent retention and unauthorized distribution of copyrighted works in digital form.

TEACH Act Implementation Guidelines

Copyright Office Circular 21: Reproduction of Copyrighted Works by Educators and Librarians

The TEACH Act is implemented through Copyright Office guidelines and institutional policies. Key implementation requirements include:

Institutional Policy Requirements
Copyright Policies: Educational institutions must institute policies regarding copyright compliance and provide accurate information about copyright laws to faculty, students, and staff.
Faculty Training: Institutions must provide informational materials that accurately describe and promote compliance with U.S. copyright laws.
Student Notification: Students must receive notice that course materials may be subject to copyright protection.
Systematic Mediated Instruction: The use of copyrighted works must be an integral part of systematic mediated instructional activities, controlled by or under the actual supervision of an instructor.
Technological Protection: For digital transmissions, institutions must apply technological measures to prevent retention beyond class sessions and unauthorized redistribution.
Access Controls: Transmissions must be limited to students officially enrolled in the course or officers/employees of governmental bodies as part of official duties.
Work Type Limitations
Nondramatic Literary Works: May be performed in their entirety (e.g., poetry, short stories, novels).
Musical Works: Nondramatic musical works may be performed in their entirety.
Dramatic Works: Only "reasonable and limited portions" may be performed (e.g., scenes from plays, operas, musicals).
Audiovisual Works: Only "reasonable and limited portions" may be displayed (e.g., clips from films, documentaries).
Visual Works: May be displayed in amounts comparable to live classroom sessions.
Technological Requirements for Digital Transmissions
  • Prevent retention of works beyond the class session
  • Prevent unauthorized further dissemination
  • Limit access to enrolled students only
  • Apply reasonable security measures
  • Avoid interference with copyright owners' technological measures
